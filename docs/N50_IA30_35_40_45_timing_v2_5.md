# AK135 CIPN Cohort 1 — IA timing comparison (30% / 35% / 40% / 45%)

**Version:** 2.5  
**Date:** 29 September 2026  
**Status:** Working analysis for discussion. IA timing is not frozen.

## 1. Fixed elements for this comparison

The following design elements are held fixed:

- Final sample size: 50/arm, total N=150.
- Primary effect: RD = p_placebo - p_treatment; positive favors AK135.
- Final promising threshold: observed RD >=10%.
- Stage 1 project rule: M=max(CP_L, CP_H); Project Go if M>=70%.
- CP projection assumptions: qP=45%, qT=30%.
- No confirmatory efficacy hypothesis testing.

Dose-level futility / drop-dose is under discussion and is **not** included in this timing comparison.

## 2. Nominal IA sample sizes and discrete CP boundary

Using round-half-up for N=50/arm:

| IA label | Nominal n/arm | Stage 1 total | Minimum placebo-active event advantage for individual CP>=70% | Nominal observed RD at boundary | CP at boundary |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30% | 15 | 45 | 2 events | 13.33% | 75.49% |
| 35% | 18 | 54 | 2 events | 11.11% | 72.73% |
| 40% | 20 | 60 | 2 events | 10.00% | 70.67% |
| 45% | 23 | 69 | 3 events | 13.04% | 76.68% |

The 45% look is not a monotone extension of the 40% look under the fixed 70% CP cutoff. Because of the discrete binary endpoint, the minimum qualifying event-count difference jumps from 2 to 3 events once the nominal arm size exceeds 20. This makes the 45% look materially more stringent than 40% despite using more data.

## 3. Exact whole-design OC

Exact binomial calculations with shared placebo were used.

### P(Stage 1 Go)

| Scenario | 30% | 35% | 40% | 45% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Null (45%,45%,45%) | 43.07% | 45.15% | 46.27% | 35.15% |
| One promising (45%,45%,35%) | 57.39% | 61.02% | 63.07% | 53.30% |
| One target (45%,45%,30%) | 66.01% | 70.33% | 72.74% | 64.78% |
| Both target (45%,30%,30%) | 76.74% | 80.93% | 83.17% | 76.93% |

### P(Stage 1 Go and Final Go)

| Scenario | 30% | 35% | 40% | 45% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Null | 19.86% | 21.29% | 22.13% | 19.60% |
| One promising | 41.46% | 44.36% | 46.02% | 41.82% |
| One target | 56.18% | 59.96% | 62.07% | 57.48% |
| Both target | 70.65% | 74.46% | 76.49% | 72.20% |

For the one-target scenario, moving from 30% to 35% adds about 3.78 percentage points in whole-design recognition probability. Moving from 35% to 40% adds about 2.11 percentage points. Moving to 45% loses performance under the fixed CP70 rule because the event-count boundary jumps from 2 to 3.

The one-target minus null separation for P(Stage 1 Go and Final Go) is approximately:

- 30%: 36.33 percentage points;
- 35%: 38.67 percentage points;
- 40%: 39.94 percentage points;
- 45%: 37.88 percentage points.

Thus 40% gives the strongest statistical separation among the four timings under the current CP70 rule, while 45% is dominated by 40% in this calibration.

## 4. Operational comparison

The current delayed-endpoint operating model is retained:

- endpoint maturation lag L=6 months;
- ordinary accrual rate r0=9/month;
- operational commitment constraint P(N_IA >=105)<=0.05.

| IA | Stage 1 total | Max post-Stage-1 accrual rate | Retained fraction of ordinary accrual | Approx. IA decision month | Approx. Go-case slowdown penalty |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30% | 45 | 7.98/month | 88.6% | 11.00 | 0.68 months |
| 35% | 54 | 6.64/month | 73.8% | 12.00 | 1.57 months |
| 40% | 60 | 5.76/month | 64.0% | 12.67 | 2.16 months |
| 45% | 69 | 4.46/month | 49.5% | 13.67 | 3.03 months |

The operational cost increases monotonically with later IA timing, whereas the statistical gain does not because of the discrete CP boundary.

## 5. Working interpretation

- **30%:** earliest and operationally lightest, but sacrifices approximately 3.8 percentage points versus 35% and 5.9 points versus 40% in one-target whole-design recognition.
- **35%:** intermediate statistical performance with materially less accrual slowdown than 40%; remains a practical working anchor.
- **40%:** strongest statistical performance among the four under the fixed CP70 rule; gain over 35% is modest (~2.1 percentage points in the one-target scenario) and comes with later decision and stronger slowdown.
- **45%:** not attractive under the current fixed CP70 rule because binary discreteness changes the qualifying event-count difference from 2 to 3. If 45% is to remain a serious candidate, the CP cutoff would need separate recalibration; that would constitute a design change rather than a pure timing comparison.

No final IA timing is selected in this note. The 35% and 40% looks remain the leading candidates for discussion.

## 6. Dose-level futility item to retain as open

A possible **strong-futility dose-level safeguard** has been discussed for the situation where one AK135 dose is clearly worse than placebo while the overall project still meets Project Go. A candidate rule considered for calibration is:

- Project Go is met;
- observed RD for dose d <= -10%; and
- CP_d <30%.

This item is not frozen and is not part of the current binding design. It should remain an Open Item rather than being described as dose selection.

## 7. Implementation note

The equal-allocation event-count thresholds above are calibration aids only. The eventual protocol/SAP implementation should continue to calculate exact CP using the realized arm-specific counts and the prespecified available-case + consumed-slot handling for permanently indeterminate Stage 1 endpoints.
