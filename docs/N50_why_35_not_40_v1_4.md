# Why 35% rather than 40% IA at N=50/arm?

**Version:** 1.4  
**Date:** 25 September 2026  
**Status:** Working rationale for IA timing; CP threshold fixed at 70% for this comparison.

## 1. Current anchor

The current sample-size anchor is:

[
N=50/	ext{arm},qquad N_{total}=150.
]

For the main Stage 1 comparison:

[
max(CP_L,CP_H)ge70%
]

is used as the Go criterion.

With equal Stage 1 allocation, both 35% and 40% IA correspond to the same attainable signal:

[
	ext{at least one AK135 dose has two fewer CIPN events than placebo.}
]

Specifically:

- 35% IA: 18/18/18 mature participants; CP at a two-event advantage = 72.7%;
- 40% IA: 20/20/20 mature participants; CP at a two-event advantage = 70.7%.

Thus the comparison is not confounded by a change from a two-event to three-event rule.

## 2. Equal-allocation statistical gain from waiting to 40%

For the key one-effective-dose scenario:

[
(Delta_L,Delta_H)=(0,15%),
]

the exact full-design OC is:

| IA | P(Stage 1 Go) | P(Stage 1 Go and Final Go) |
|---:|---:|---:|
|35%|70.33%|59.96%|
|40%|72.74%|62.07%|

Therefore waiting from 35% to 40% gains:

[
+2.41	ext{ percentage points}
]

in Stage 1 continuation and:

[
oxed{+2.11	ext{ percentage points}}
]

in the unconditional probability of both passing Stage 1 and ultimately meeting the final promising criterion.

For the both-target scenario ((15%,15%)):

- 35% IA: 74.46% ultimately pass Stage 1 and meet final promising;
- 40% IA: 76.49%.

The gain is again approximately 2 percentage points.

For the null scenario, the probability of both passing Stage 1 and meeting the final promising criterion also rises slightly:

- 35% IA: 21.29%;
- 40% IA: 22.13%.

Thus the improvement in separation between the one-target scenario and the null is only about:

[
(62.07-22.13)-(59.96-21.29)
=
1.27	ext{ percentage points}.
]

## 3. Robustness to mild Stage 1 arm imbalance

The equal-n mnemonic of "two fewer events" should not be used as the actual protocol rule. The actual rule remains the exact CP calculated from the observed arm-specific mature sample sizes.

To test robustness without assuming a particular randomization algorithm, deterministic imbalance envelopes were examined.

### +/-1 participant per arm

For 35% IA, total Stage 1 N remains 54 and allocations are restricted to 17-19 per arm.

For 40% IA, total Stage 1 N remains 60 and allocations are restricted to 19-21 per arm.

Because it is unknown which dose is truly effective, the one-effective-dose result below uses the less favorable of the "Low only effective" and "High only effective" scenarios for each allocation.

| IA | P(Stage 1 Go), one-effective-dose worst-case range | P(Stage 1 Go and Final Go), one-effective-dose worst-case range |
|---:|---:|---:|
|35%|61.8%-70.3%|53.8%-60.0%|
|40%|64.4%-72.7%|56.3%-62.1%|

The ranges overlap substantially. The 40% look shifts the lower end upward by roughly 2.5 percentage points, but does not create a qualitatively different decision performance.

### +/-2 participant per arm stress envelope

| IA | P(Stage 1 Go), one-effective-dose worst-case range | P(Stage 1 Go and Final Go), one-effective-dose worst-case range |
|---:|---:|---:|
|35%|61.8%-72.5%|53.8%-61.5%|
|40%|62.4%-72.7%|54.7%-62.1%|

Under this wider stress envelope, the two IA timings are almost completely overlapping.

This suggests that realistic mild arm imbalance does not provide a strong statistical reason to delay the IA from 35% to 40%.

The exact randomization method has not been assumed here. If a restricted/block randomization is ultimately specified, the realized imbalance may be smaller than these stress envelopes.

Under the deterministic Go-case approximation, the calendar penalty due to slowdown is approximately:

- 35% IA: 1.57 months;
- 40% IA: 2.16 months.

## 5. Working rationale for preferring 35%

The comparison can therefore be stated as:

> Waiting from 35% to 40% mature information increases the unconditional probability of identifying a one-target-dose program by only about 2 percentage points under equal allocation. Mild arm imbalance does not materially change this conclusion. In contrast, the 40% look requires a stronger recruitment slowdown and produces a later interim decision under the current delayed-endpoint operational model.

Therefore, under the current sample-size and recruitment constraints:

[
oxed{35%	ext{ IA is the preferred working timing}}
]

with 40% retained as a sensitivity/alternative timing rather than the primary design.

This preference is operationally driven but is supported by the finding that the incremental statistical gain from 40% is small.

## 6. Important implementation point

The protocol/SAP should define the Stage 1 criterion using exact conditional power based on the **actual mature sample size in each randomized arm**, not using the equal-allocation shorthand of "two fewer events."

The equal-n event-count rule is only an interpretation aid for design calibration.
