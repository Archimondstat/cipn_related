# External evidence for the 30% active-arm planning rate

**Version:** 1.7  
**Date:** 25 September 2026  
**Status:** Working rationale for the Cohort 1 design alternative.

## 1. Question

AK135 currently has Phase I dose-escalation experience but no efficacy data in the planned prophylactic CIPN setting. Therefore:

[
p_T^{design}=30%
]

must not be presented as an estimate of the expected AK135 event rate.

The design question is narrower:

> Is a 30% CTCAE grade >=2 CIPN event rate, under a central placebo assumption of 45%, a clinically meaningful and externally plausible Phase II target scenario?

The corresponding difference:

[
45%-30%=15%
]

is a **derived design-alternative risk difference**, not an assumed invariant pharmacologic effect.

## 2. Most relevant external evidence

### 2.1 IDEA: control-rate anchor, not an intervention-effect estimate

The IDEA pooled analysis included 12,834 patients with stage III colon cancer. Among patients receiving 6 months of FOLFOX, grade >=2 neurotoxicity during active treatment and the month after treatment occurred in 47.7%.

This is highly relevant to the current planned population/exposure and supports 45% as a reasonable central control-rate assumption.

However, the current AK135 endpoint extends through 3 months after the actual last mFOLFOX6 dose, so the endpoint windows are not identical.

Reference: Grothey et al. N Engl J Med. 2018;378:1177-1188. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1713709.

### 2.2 ART-123 Phase IIa: strongest directly comparable positive signal

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase IIa trial enrolled stage II/III colon cancer patients receiving 12 cycles of adjuvant mFOLFOX6.

At cycle 12, cumulative NCI-CTCAE grade >=2 sensory neuropathy was:

- placebo: 64.3% (n=28);
- 1-day ART-123: 40.7% (n=27);
- 3-day ART-123: 45.8% (n=24).

The observed absolute differences versus placebo were therefore approximately:

[
23.6	ext{ percentage points}
]

and

[
18.5	ext{ percentage points},
]

respectively.

The comparisons were not statistically significant for this CTCAE endpoint:

- 1-day ART-123: 95% CI for difference approximately -48.4 to 4.0 percentage points, P=0.108;
- 3-day ART-123: 95% CI approximately -44.2 to 9.4 percentage points, P=0.264.

This study is important because the population, 12-cycle mFOLFOX6 background, prevention setting, and CTCAE grade >=2 endpoint are close to the current design.

It shows that an observed absolute reduction larger than 15 percentage points is **not unprecedented** in a highly comparable randomized setting.

It does **not** establish that AK135 should be expected to achieve a 15-point reduction, and it does not directly support an active-arm incidence of exactly 30%.

Reference: A placebo-controlled, double-blind, randomized study of recombinant thrombomodulin (ART-123) to prevent oxaliplatin-induced peripheral neuropathy. doi:10.1007/s00280-020-04135-8; PMID 32965539.

## 3. Important negative comparators

### 3.1 GM1 Phase III

A randomized placebo-controlled double-blind Phase III study enrolled 196 stage II/III CRC patients receiving adjuvant mFOLFOX6.

NCI-CTCAE grade >=2 cumulative neurotoxicity was:

- GM1: 33.7%;
- placebo: 31.6%;
- P=0.76.

Thus a neuroprotective intervention with a plausible mechanism produced no cumulative CTCAE benefit in a directly relevant population.

Reference: Wang et al. Cancer Med. 2020;9:151-159. doi:10.1002/cam4.2693; PMID 31724334.

### 3.2 GENIUS Phase III

This randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study evaluated Goshajinkigan during adjuvant mFOLFOX6.

Among evaluable patients, grade >=2 neurotoxicity occurred in:

- Goshajinkigan: 50.6%;
- placebo: 31.2%.

The trial was stopped early; Cox HR for neuropathy was 1.908 (P=0.007).

This is a reminder that prevention interventions can be neutral or harmful and that promising biological rationale does not reliably translate into clinical prevention.

Reference: Oki et al. Int J Clin Oncol. 2015;20:767-775. doi:10.1007/s10147-015-0784-9; PMID 25627820.

### 3.3 PC-SOD Phase II

A double-blind placebo-controlled randomized Phase II trial enrolled 90 stage II/III CRC patients scheduled for 12 cycles of mFOLFOX6.

For the primary endpoint, time/cycles to NCI-CTCAE grade >=2 CIPN:

[
HR=0.88,quad 95%CI 0.44-1.78,quad P=0.72.
]

The trial therefore did not demonstrate the target primary-endpoint benefit, although secondary/exploratory neuropathy measures showed more favorable signals.

Reference: Yamada et al. ESMO Gastrointestinal Oncology. 2025;10:100269. doi:10.1016/j.esmogo.2025.100269.

### 3.4 POLAR-A/POLAR-M

In the combined analysis of the two Phase III calmangafodipir trials, moderate-to-severe CIPN at month 9 occurred in:

- calmangafodipir 5 micromol/kg: 54.3%;
- placebo: 40.3%.

Estimated RR was 1.37 (95% CI 1.01-1.86; P=0.045), favoring placebo.

The endpoint is not the same CTCAE binary endpoint as the current trial, but the result further shows that a preventive intervention can perform worse than placebo.

Reference: Glimelius et al. J Natl Cancer Inst. 2023;115:205-212. PMID 36308441.

### 3.5 Alliance A221805 duloxetine Phase II

This randomized double-blind placebo-controlled Phase II study enrolled 199 participants with stage II/III colorectal cancer receiving oxaliplatin.

The primary endpoint was a participant-reported composite response rather than CTCAE grade >=2 CIPN.

Response rates were:

- placebo: 68.0%;
- duloxetine 30 mg: 65.2%;
- duloxetine 60 mg: 66.0%.

The study did not identify a promising prophylactic duloxetine dose.

Because the endpoint differs substantially, this study is qualitative context rather than a quantitative anchor for the current 30% rate.

Reference: Alliance A221805. JCO Oncology Advances. 2026. doi:10.1200/oa-25-00107; PMID 42052361.

## 4. Broader evidence

A 2025 systematic review identified 20 randomized studies of preventive interventions in CRC patients receiving oxaliplatin, with a median sample size of 61 and substantial heterogeneity.

The review reported an apparently large pooled reduction in end-of-treatment grade >=2 OIPN for anti-oxidative-stress interventions, but:

- individual trials were generally small;
- 35% of studies were judged at potential high risk of bias;
- only selected intervention classes were poolable;
- endpoint timing and interventions varied.

Therefore the pooled estimate should not be used to derive a precise AK135 event-rate assumption.

Reference: Salama et al. Front Oncol. 2025;15:1642552. doi:10.3389/fonc.2025.1642552; PMID 41001014.

## 5. What the external evidence supports

The literature does **not** support the statement:

> AK135 is expected to reduce CTCAE grade >=2 CIPN incidence to 30%.

There is no AK135 efficacy evidence in this prophylactic setting.

The literature supports the more limited statement:

> In closely related randomized mFOLFOX6 prevention studies, absolute differences of approximately 18-24 percentage points have been observed as positive Phase II signals, while multiple other interventions have shown no benefit or harm. Therefore a 15-percentage-point difference is clinically ambitious but not outside the range of effects observed in this development area.

ART-123 is the clearest external precedent for the plausibility of the **magnitude** of the 15-point design alternative.

The negative GM1, Goshajinkigan, PC-SOD and POLAR results are equally important: they prevent the 15-point alternative from being interpreted as an expected or conservative effect.

## 6. Recommended interpretation of the working rates

Use the following terminology:

[
oxed{p_P^{design}=45%}
]

**Central placebo/control planning incidence.**

[
oxed{p_T^{design}=30%}
]

**Target active-arm planning incidence / design alternative.**

[
oxed{Delta^{design}=15%}
]

**Absolute risk difference implied by the central planning rates.**

[
oxed{Delta_{promising}=10%}
]

**Final observed-effect threshold for a promising efficacy signal.**

[
oxed{Delta_{weak}=5%}
]

**Weak-effect boundary.**

The 30% rate is therefore an aspirational but externally plausible Phase II target, not an evidence-based forecast of AK135 efficacy.

## 7. Consequence for the design

The current central assumptions can be retained provisionally:

[
45%ightarrow30%
]

provided the protocol/design rationale makes the distinction above explicit.

The design should continue to show operating characteristics across a joint range of placebo and treatment incidences, because neither 45% nor 30% is known precisely before this trial.

The 10% promising threshold is especially important because it prevents the entire development decision from depending on the stronger 15-point central alternative.
